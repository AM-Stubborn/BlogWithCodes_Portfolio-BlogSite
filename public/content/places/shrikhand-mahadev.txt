This trek was never really planned. I already had other things to do on 25 June. But Vishal was coming specially for Shrikhand, so Harish and I were ready to go.

First we had to drop Buggu at his bua’s place in Phagwara, and then come back. We left at 7 in the evening on 24 June and reached Phagwara around 9:30. The road was very dark. At a crossing, Harish was driving when a milkman’s cart came out of nowhere and hit the car. It was a small accident. We asked after the man, he was alright, and a few minutes later we moved on.

We left Phagwara around 10 and drove straight to Chandigarh airport for Vishal. His flight was supposed to land at 1 in the morning. It was late by an hour and a half, so he reached at 2:30. Then we started for the hills.

Harish took the wheel. I stretched out in the back on an air mattress. It had a slow puncture. Every hour the air would leak out, and I had to fill it again. Around Kumarsain, Harish was sleepy too. We stopped, all of us slept for a bit, drove a little, then stopped and slept again. That is how we reached Rampur. After Rampur everyone was awake, and we went on to Dev Dhank.

Dev Dhank is a temple just before Nirmand. People visit it before Shri khand. The one-kilometre road up to the temple was in bad shape, but we made it, and the darshan was good. In Nirmand we had parathas for breakfast, and then we drove to Jaon, the village where the Shrikhand trail starts.

In Jaon we drove until the road ended. There was a homestay there. We parked the car and finally got some proper rest. Vishal and I were still working that day. We were in meetings until 4 in the evening. At 4, we picked up our bags and started walking.

The pace was good at first. Before Shinghard we left the trail by mistake. We had gone about 500 metres the wrong way, so it cost us an extra kilometre. After that we found the path again and walked well until Barati Nala.

After Barati Nala it became very hard for me. The climb was steep, and for about four kilometres I was done. Sweat was running off me, and I was badly dehydrated. At the first shelter we saw, I went in and lay down. It was not rest. I blacked out. I was out for about twenty minutes. When I woke, Vishal told me to eat. We had rice and rajma. As soon as I finished, I threw it all up. Even so, I felt a little better.

I thought we would sleep there. Vishal had a different plan. If we could climb a few more kilometres that evening, the next day would be easier. We had planned to reach Parvati Bagh the next day, no matter what. I got up. This time Vishal carried my bag, and I climbed with only my own weight. Even that was too much. After two or three kilometres I was out of breath again. Vishal said we should stop for the night, about two kilometres before Thachru. I ate dinner again, because the first one had not stayed down. Thank you, Vishal, for carrying that bag.

Next morning we had no breakfast, only a cup of tea, and we started up. I was in better shape. We climbed slowly and reached Kali Top in about two hours. From there we saw Mahadev for the first time. We prayed, and then we ate breakfast.

After Kali Top I walked in front. I knew Vishal and Harish could catch me any time. The trail went down to Bheem Talai, so my pace was fine. From there to Kunsa it climbed steeply again. I slowed, and Harish took my bag for about a kilometre. He carried it in front of him, and the uphill upset his stomach. After Kunsa the trail felt normal again. I got my pace back. Harish did not.

We reached Bheem Duar around 3 in the afternoon. Parvati Bagh was still a steep two kilometres ahead. We ate lunch and started again around 3. On the way we crossed a beautiful waterfall. The trail beside it did not feel safe. Then the climb to Parvati Bagh began. Harish and I were both struggling. We had to rest every fifty steps or so. Vishal went ahead to book a tent. We reached around 5, had a few snacks, and lay down. The last stretch was in the morning.

Dinner was around 8. About an hour later the camp owner came to our tent and shouted that the trail would be closed the next day. The ropes tied on the snow for the climb had been removed. He said not to go up, no matter what. That made us sad, and none of us could sleep after that. We had come this far only to reach the top and have darshan. The plan was still to wake at 2:30. If other people were going up, we would go with them. Even so, we were ready to go anyway.

At 2:30 we saw a few people getting ready. That cheered us up. We got ready too and left around 3. I knew it would be hard. I did not know how hard.

For the first kilometre Vishal stayed beside me and kept me moving. I was very slow, and Harish was not well either, so Vishal went ahead. That was my mistake. The bag with our snacks, water, and medicine was with him. Harish and I had nothing to drink.

Snow was everywhere. We were walking on the glacier. Harish threw up a couple of times. I was fine, only slow, because the height was making it hard to breathe. A few other climbers shared water and medicine with us. That helped. We did not turn around. We kept going up.

I gave myself a simple rule. Take at least twenty steps, then rest. Sometimes I managed thirty. Twenty was the least I would do. Near the top, Harish found some strength, picked up his pace, and reached before me. I stayed with my twenty steps. I was on top around 7:30 in the morning. I took one sip of water and threw up everything. We stayed about fifteen minutes. A quick darshan, and that was all. The view on the other side of the peak was beautiful.

I thought coming down would be easy. I was wrong. My body had nothing left. I was moving only on willpower. I thought I could slide on the snow and save energy. The first slide showed me how bad that idea was. If the slope got steeper, I might not be able to stop. So I decided not to slide. My legs did not listen. I kept tripping, and I slid a few times anyway. Each time I had my stick, and I used it to stop.

One moment is still clear. I was trying to come down carefully. I meant to place my foot at an angle, but my mind and my body were not working together. With no strength left, I put my foot straight down. I slipped. The slope took me, and suddenly I was sliding. I tried to stay calm and pushed the stick into the snow with whatever I had. The incline was too much. Then a hump in the snow threw me over. I flipped. My face was toward the slope and my legs were in the air. For a moment I thought I was gone.

When something like that happens, time slows down. I saw a rope a little away from me, one of the ropes fixed for people climbing up. Catching it was the only chance I had. I do not know how I reached it. Somehow it was in my hand, and I held on as hard as I could. I had slid at least 200 metres before I stopped.

Vishal could see the whole thing. All he could say was, “Harish, Manish gaya.” He could not do anything else. I sat there until both of them reached me.

From there we came down holding the rope. The snow was melting, and walking on it was hard. We slipped a few more times, but with the rope in hand we could stop ourselves. Somehow we got back to Parvati Bagh.

Just before the camp, my mouth was so dry I could not even make saliva. That is how dehydrated I was. In the dhaba, Vishal and I fell asleep without meaning to. We woke up two hours later, walked a little, and ate lunch.

Around 2 we started down. The height was wearing us out. By the time we reached Bheem Duar, the headache felt normal again. We crossed Kunsa quickly. After Kunsa a little rain started. Past Bheem Talai there was a steep climb of about 500 metres. This time I was not gasping. I just kept a slow, steady pace.

At first everyone wanted to stay at Kali Top. I was feeling strong in my head and wanted to go all the way down to the homestay. My mind was ready. My body was not. It had no energy left. Coming down from Kali Top I tripped a few times, and I knew going all the way down would not be easy. We spent the night at Thachru.

I threw up before dinner, then I ate. While I was sleeping I saw that my clothes were wet through with sweat. In the morning, at 7, we left without breakfast. We walked well, came down to Barati Nala, waited a few minutes, and went back to the car.

We sat at the parking for ten or fifteen minutes, then started for home. Somewhere we took a wrong road and lost some time. In the end we stopped at a hotel in Rampur and ate a proper meal, breakfast and lunch together. After that we dropped Vishal at Theog. Harish and I drove back to Chandigarh the same day.

This whole trek was a once in a lifetime experience. It made me realise that if you decide to achieve something, it is not impossible. You just have to keep going. After this, I am already looking for the next trek.

Jai Mahadev.
